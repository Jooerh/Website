Bell to Bell Overnight

A long-only strategy for the overnight session in Nasdaq-100 futures. It takes at most one trade a night, sends a far-away disaster stop with the entry, and is flat before the 9:30 ET open. On most nights it doesn't trade.

On your chart

What you'll see, in order

Once it's on, it runs by itself: it waits, trades and goes flat on its own. The exact rules and settings are proprietary. This is the behavior you'll see, and the limits fixed in the code.

  1. Waits

    Each night starts flat. On most nights its conditions never line up, and it doesn't trade.

  2. Enters

    When they do, it buys, at most once a night, with a disaster stop far from the market.

  3. Holds

    It holds through the overnight session. Most trades end with a small loss, and the winners are far larger.

  4. Exits

    When its rules end the trade, or at the latest before the 9:30 ET open.

  5. Flat

    It never carries a position into the regular session or over a weekend.

Weak spots

  • Chosen on this same history. Its rules were found on the data shown here, and later checks confirmed only part of them. One of its rules held up on MNQ but not clearly on NQ.
  • Expect less than the MNQ figures. On NQ the same rules made a profit factor of 2.05. Expect results closer to that than to MNQ's 3.03.
  • Uneven on NQ. On NQ it has had losing stretches of a year or more.
  • Most trades lose. About three in four trades lose after costs, most of them by a little.
  • Overnight risk. It holds positions through the overnight session, including early-morning US economic reports. Prices can gap, and an exit can fill well away from where the trade turned.
  • A far-away stop. Its only stop at entry is far from the market, so a single loss can be large. Its worst NQ trade lost 501 points, $10,020 at 1 NQ. It was tested at one contract, and more contracts multiply its worst nights.

How it was tested

  • Data: clean NQ and MNQ price data, Eastern time. MNQ from May 2019 and NQ from Jan 2018, both to Oct 2026.
  • Costs: 1 point a round trip ($2 on MNQ, $20 on NQ). Your costs may be higher.
  • Size: one contract on every trade, no compounding.
  • Against chance: trades on randomly chosen nights, entered the same way, earned about 1.5 points each, against 17.5 for its own picks on MNQ and 10.6 on NQ.
  • Tighter stops: in testing, a tighter stop at entry lowered its MNQ profit factor to between 1.49 and 1.66.
  • Code check: in offline tests, the strategy file matched the research model trade for trade across 40 test runs.

How this is worked out
  • Every trading day with at least 3 months of backtest after it starts its own try, walked forward one trading day at a time on the rules shown for the time allowed, so every setting uses the same start dates. A try that neither passes nor fails in that time counts as neither; one the backtest ends before counts as still running.
  • Each day’s lowest point counts open trades at each minute’s worst price. A run fails when that point touches the floor, which is checked before that day’s close is checked against the profit target.
  • End-of-day trailing: the floor follows the highest closing balance, a drawdown below it, and stops rising at the start + $100. Intraday trailing: it follows the highest balance reached during the day, open trades counted at each minute’s best price, and stops at the same level. Static: the floor never moves.
  • Daily loss limit: half the account’s drawdown. When a day’s loss reaches it, the day either ends there, closed at the limit, or the account fails, as picked.
  • Consistency rule: the profit target only counts once the best day is no more than the share picked of the total profit. Minimum trading days: days with at least one trade.
  • Worse fills: every entry and exit a tick worse than the backtest’s (an extra $1 a contract on each trade). The board also reruns your setup with worse fills and shows the difference under the result.
  • At these sizes: the strategies picked, at the contracts picked, over the whole test period, after the research costs and before any firm’s rules (no daily loss limit), with worse fills when picked. $ a week and trades a week divide by the test period’s calendar weeks. Select’s figures elsewhere count only the sessions with complete data, so its pace there runs a little higher than here. A week runs Monday to Friday and counts the trades that closed in it. A losing day is a day with an entry or an exit whose closed trades came to less than zero; days with neither don’t count and don’t break a run. Weeks work the same way.
  • Worst day and worst five trading days: the deepest point the strategies picked reached together inside one trading day, and inside any five in a row, counted from where that stretch began, with open trades marked.
  • Firm plans: the plan’s own target, drawdown, daily loss limit, consistency rule and minimum days for the size picked, and for After you pass its funded account and first-payout rules, as each firm’s pages showed them on Nov 17 2026. What the replay can’t follow (a limit that grows, news windows, contract limits and scaling) is listed under the plan. Enter your own: the same replay on the numbers you type, in $50 steps.
  • The figures come from the research backtests after their costs. The board only ever gets back totals and worst cases, never a single trade or when it happened.
